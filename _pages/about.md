## About Me
At CMU, I lead the policy program at the Center for Informed Democracy and Social-cybersecurity ([IDeaS](https://www.cmu.edu/ideas-social-cybersecurity/index.html)). My current research categorizes and analyzes the failure modes of AI-generated search summaries, such as Google’s AI Overviews, and investigates possible technical and legal policy responses that could address these failures. In 2026, I led the conference organizing committee for the IDeaS Conference on “Governing the Digital Age: AI, Platforms, and Online Harms Policy,” and I am co-editing a related [special issue](https://link.springer.com/collections/aejhahchjj) of <i>Computational and Mathematical Organization Theory</i>.

My [PhD dissertation](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/thesis/Effective_and_Practical_Strategies_for_Combatting_Misinformation/29316179?file=55375001) focused on developing effective and practical countermeasures to misinformation. While there has been substantial work on analyzing the effectiveness of various interventions, little work has been conducted on **user acceptance** — a critical component for implementation. 
I studied interventions ranging from individual-level actions (e.g., reporting misinformation on social media) to platform- and government-level policies. In the course of my work, I identified understudied strategies, evaluated user behavior and perceptions, and developed evaluation frameworks that consider both effectiveness and user acceptance. Some of my [research](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/misinformation-desk/202510/you-are-the-fact-checker-youre-waiting-for) has been highlighted in Psychology Today, a popular science magazine. 

Before CMU, I earned a BS in [Mathematics](https://www.wm.edu/as/mathematics/) and an MS in Computational Operations Research at William & Mary. My undergraduate thesis, ["Nonlinear Models of Zooplankton Communities"](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.12781.54242), was completed with [Prof. Sarah Day](http://www.math.wm.edu/~sday/) and [Prof. Drew LaMar](https://www.wm.edu/as/biology/people/faculty/lamar_d.php). For my Master’s capstone, I worked with [Prof. Lawrence M. Leemis](http://www.math.wm.edu/~leemis/) to develop a [simulation](https://faster-voting.wm.edu) that optimized resource allocation to lower voting times on Election Day. After my Master's, I worked in industry (including as a Data Scientist at [MITRE](https://www.mitre.org)) before pursuing my PhD.
